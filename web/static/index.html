Upagrahas and outer planets

What the upagrahas mean

Upagrahas ("sub-planets") are sensitive points, not bodies in the sky. Five follow from the Sun's longitude; the other six come from the eighths of the day or night, each ruled by a planet, and take the Lagna at that moment. Classically they are malefic and harm the house they occupy, above all when they join the Lagna, the Sun or the Moon. Yamaghaṇṭaka is the benign exception. They refine a reading; they don't outweigh the planets. The app shows them for reference only and uses them in no calculation.

Gulika (Gk)
The "son of Saturn" and the most used upagraha. Shows where obstacles, delays, fear and karmic burdens sit; its sign lord matters for health and longevity.
Māndi (Md)
Often treated as the same point as Gulika (here: the middle of Saturn's part). Hardship, chronic trouble, poison in the classical sense.
Kāla (Kl)
Sun's part, "time". Misfortune, delays, endings.
Mṛtyu (Mr)
Mars' part, "death". Accidents, injury, sudden danger.
Ardhaprahara (Ap)
Mercury's part, "half watch". Obstacles in work, trade and communication.
Yamaghaṇṭaka (Yk)
Jupiter's part. Rather benign, acts like a mild Jupiter and favours undertakings.
Dhūma (Dh)
"Smoke". Heat, fire, anger, grief.
Vyatīpāta (Vy)
"Calamity". Sudden reversals and accidents.
Pariveṣa (Pv)
"Halo" around Sun or Moon. Mixed and milder; some read it as protection.
Indrachāpa (Ic)
"Rainbow". Mixed: show and illusion, sometimes deception.
Upaketu (Uk)
"Comet", acts like Ketu. Sudden loss, separation, detachment. Always 30° behind the Sun.

Common Parashari yogas from the D1 (whole-sign houses, graha drishti), plus Vipareeta and Parivartana yogas in D9 and D10. Conventions vary by school; a study aid, not a verdict.

Chara Dasha

Annual chart (Tajika) cast for the moment the Sun returns to its natal sidereal position, at the birthplace. Muntha moves one sign per year from the natal Lagna.

The Vedic day (vara) runs from sunrise to sunrise, so a birth before sunrise counts to the previous weekday.

Favourable windows

Computed for the chart's place. Each window is when the favourable nakshatra is active that day (local time); tithi, yoga and karana are taken near sunrise. Rikta tithis, Amavasya, Vishti karana and malefic yogas lower the rating. A study aid, not a final muhurta.

Sidereal (Lahiri) position of the eclipsed light: the Sun for solar, the Moon for lunar eclipses. Hits lists natal points (Lagna and the nine grahas) the eclipse aspects within the orb: ☌ conjunction, ☍ opposition, □ square, with the exact distance. Visibility is for Switzerland.

Sarvashtakavarga: total points per sign. 30 or more is strong, 26 to 29 average, 25 or less weak (the average is 28).

Bhinnashtakavarga per planet

Per planet: 5 or more is strong, 4 average, 3 or less weak. Underlined: the planet's natal sign.

Six-fold strength in rupas (1 rupa = 60 virupas). A planet is strong when it reaches its classical minimum (the marker on each bar).

Bhava Bala: house strengths

Per house: the lord's Shad Bala plus the house's directional and aspect strength. Cheshta and parts of Kala bala are approximated.

Ashtakoota (Guna Milan): the classical 36-point Moon match between this chart and a partner.
